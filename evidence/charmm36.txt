CHARMM36: what is now convertible, and what still cannot be checked
===================================================================

Work package 10 asks for one of two things: a complete CHARMM36 entry in the
evidence folder, or a reviewed correction to the claim.

This is the second. The terms are convertible and the converter now converts
them. A validated deck is not produced, because there is no way to produce a
reference energy to validate it against, and the reason is in the force-field
file itself rather than in anything MOSAICS does.

Nothing here is taken from a report. Every statement below names the file it
was read out of or the command that produced it.


C-01  The deck had no van der Waals at all                          FIXED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A CHARMM XML splits the non-bonded term across two elements. NonbondedForce
carries the charge; LennardJonesForce carries sigma and epsilon.

  charmm36.xml, NonbondedForce
    412 Atom entries, every one of them epsilon="0.0" sigma="1.0"

  charmm36.xml, LennardJonesForce
    412 Atom entries with the real parameters, 14 NBFixPair

The converter read both from NonbondedForce, so the charmm36 preset wrote
85,078 ~inter_parm lines all reading \eps{0}\sig{10}, and the manifest said
nbfix_pairs 0.

Now: 84,255 of 85,078 pairs carry a non-zero epsilon, and the 823 that do not
are pairs where a type's own epsilon is genuinely zero.

This was never nucleic-specific. It broke every CHARMM-family XML the
converter could be pointed at.


C-02  Urey-Bradley                                                  FIXED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1497 entries, 810 unordered triples. Carried in ~bend_parm's own bond slot,
which bend.cpp already evaluates as 0.5*fk_bond*(r13-eq_bond)^2. No engine
change. 807 emitted.

Three dropped, and counted in the manifest. Four triples carry a negative
force constant -- P-ON2-P and its P3 variants -- which CHARMM allows and
database.cpp:706 rejects for the whole deck. They are pyrophosphate linkages
and do not occur in a phosphodiester backbone.


C-03  Harmonic impropers                                            FIXED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
188 entries, k*(psi-psi0)^2. Carried as ~torsion_parm \pot_type{harm} with
\fk and \eq, which database.cpp expands into the same power series the cosine
form uses. No engine change.

The expansion is a truncated sixth-order Fourier series, not the exact
quadratic. Measured against 0.5*fk*dphi^2:

    10 degrees from eq      exact to four figures
    30 degrees              -0.2 per cent
    45 degrees              -1.8 per cent
    60 degrees              -7.7 per cent

Any cross-engine tolerance on a system with soft impropers has to budget for
that. Nucleobase impropers are stiff and stay inside 20 degrees.


C-04  CMAP                                                     UNSUPPORTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Eight maps, sixteen CMAPTorsionForce children. Every selector is protein
backbone, and two of them distinguish a proline neighbour by the class of the
FOLLOWING residue -- NH1 against N. ~cmap_binding selects by resname and has
no way to say "unless the next one is proline". Changing that changes the file
format and the engine, so it is a decision and not a conversion.

Not on the nucleic path. No nucleic residue in CHARMM36 uses CMAP.


C-05  No deck is committed, and this is why                    MEASURABLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A deck is worth committing when it can be checked. This one cannot be, and the
obstacle is in charmm36.xml.

Its five nucleic templates -- ADE GUA CYT THY URA -- each declare exactly one
external bond:

    ADE: ExternalBond=["O3'"]  AllowPatch=['DEO5', 'DEOX', 'MET2']
    GUA: ExternalBond=["O3'"]  AllowPatch=['DEO5', 'DEOX', 'MET2']
    CYT: ExternalBond=["O3'"]  AllowPatch=['DEO5', 'DEOX', 'MET2']
    THY: ExternalBond=["O3'"]  AllowPatch=['DEO5', 'DEOX', 'MET2']
    URA: ExternalBond=["O3'"]  AllowPatch=['DEO5', 'DEOX', 'MET2']

Each owns its own 5-phosphate and declares no external bond on P. In a strand
every internal residue needs two: its P to the previous O3', and its own O3'
to the next P. Only the 5-most residue could ever match, and it cannot either,
because matching it would mean removing the phosphate and

    of the 107 patches in charmm36.xml, none is a 5-prime patch.

So the nucleic block of charmm36.xml describes mononucleotides. It cannot
build a strand, which means OpenMM cannot produce a reference energy for one,
which means a converted deck has nothing to be checked against.

charmm36_2024.xml does not have this problem: its templates declare
ExternalBond on both O3' and P, and it ships 5TER, 5MET, 5PHO and 5POM. It is
a different parameter release and a much harder conversion target -- 100,295
atom types against 412, and its impropers are generated at load time by an
embedded script rather than declared in the XML, so the converter cannot see
them at all.

Producing a validated CHARMM36 nucleic deck therefore needs a decision that
has not been taken:

  (a) use charmm36_2024.xml for the reference energies, accepting that it is
      a different release from the parameters being converted;
  (b) repair charmm36.xml locally -- add ExternalBond on P to the five
      templates and author the missing 5-prime patches -- and say in the
      provenance that the reference came from a modified file;
  (c) go through CHARMM or ParmEd with a .psf and par_all36_na.prm, and leave
      the OpenMM XML out of the reference path entirely.

None of these is a conversion problem. All three are yours.


C-08  A reference energy now exists, and which terminus carries it  FIXED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-05 said a converted deck would have nothing to be checked against.
charmm36_2024.xml can produce a reference, and does:

  examples/pure_terminal_controls/rna_acgu.pdb, 127 atoms, 5TER/3TER

    term                    kcal/mol   OpenMM force
    ------------------------------------------------------------
    bond                 28.5150180595   HarmonicBondForce
    Urey-Bradley (1-3)   27.3393941982   HarmonicBondForce
    angle               166.3612094821   HarmonicAngleForce
    torsion             114.0295240127   PeriodicTorsionForce
    improper (harmonic)   0.0010225163   CustomTorsionForce
    1-4 Lennard-Jones   230.8957510555   CustomBondForce
    Lennard-Jones      2564.7497007603   CustomNonbondedForce
    electrostatics     -119.9844133973   NonbondedForce
    correction map        0.0000000000   CMAPTorsionForce
    ------------------------------------------------------------
    TOTAL              3011.9072066874

  THE CLASS NAME IS NOT THE TERM, AND READING IT AS ONE IS WRONG. Two of
  these were mislabelled when this section was first written:

    CustomBondForce is the 1-4 Lennard-Jones, not Urey-Bradley. Its energy
    expression is 4*epsilon*((sigma/r)^12-(sigma/r)^6) and none of its 320
    pairs is a bond in the topology.

    Urey-Bradley has no force of its own in OpenMM. CHARMM's 1-3 harmonic
    goes into HarmonicBondForce beside the real bonds, so the single
    HarmonicBondForce energy of 55.8544122578 was two terms added together:
    136 topology bonds and 42 one-three pairs. MOSAICS carries the 1-3 part
    in the bend record's own bond slot, so a term-by-term comparison needs
    them apart, and the tool now separates them. 28.5150180595 +
    27.3393941982 returns the original 55.8544122578.

  tools/charmm_reference_energy.py, on the committed control with no second
  copy of it: the tool renames HO5' and HO3' to CHARMM's H5T and H3T itself.

CMAP is 0.0000000000 on a nucleic strand, which measures what C-04 says
rather than asserting it.

WHY THIS DID NOT WORK BEFORE, AND IT WAS NOT THE CHEMISTRY

OpenMM builds intra-residue bonds from its own table of standard residue
names. RA5, RC, RG and RU3 are not in it, so every atom of the first residue
came back with no bonds at all, and template matching cannot succeed for an
unbonded residue. What it printed was

  No template found for residue 0 (ADE). The set of atoms is similar to OMA,
  but is missing 1 H atom, 1 C atom, 2 O atoms, and 1 P atom.

which reads as a statement about a missing phosphate and is not one. It is
what an unbonded residue looks like. The bonds are now built from the force
field's own residue templates, with the patch's own AddBond and RemoveBond
applied, plus the O3'-to-P backbone links.

WHICH TERMINUS CAN END A CHAIN

Tested, once the bonds were real:

  5TER   5-prime-OH          BUILD OK, 127 particles
  5PHO   5-prime-phosphate   BUILD FAILED

The atom sets are not the problem. A 5-prime-phosphate residue built by
tools/build_charmm_nucleic_reference.py has exactly the 35 atoms of ADE+5PHO,
with nothing on either side the other lacks, and geometry taken from the
strand's own internal phosphate: P-O5' 1.600 A, P-O5T 1.610 A, no contact
closer than 1.600 A.

It fails because 5PHO carries no RemoveExternalBond element, while 5TER and
5MET each carry one. ADE declares external bonds on both O3' and P, so after
5PHO the phosphorus still requires a partner that a chain end does not have.

  patch    RemoveExternalBond elements     keeps the phosphate
  5TER     1                               no
  5MET     1                               no
  5PHO     0                               yes
  5POM     0                               yes

Every patch that ends a chain removes the phosphate, and every patch that
keeps the phosphate cannot end a chain. So a bare 5-prime-phosphate terminus
is not expressible in this file, and the reference is built on 5-prime-OH.

1EFS IS AN EXAMPLE OF THE TERMINUS THAT CANNOT BE EXPRESSED

The RNA strand of examples/1efs_heteroduplex/1efs.pdb opens with P, O1P and
O2P and no capping oxygen: a bare 5-prime phosphate. Running the tool on it
reports those three atoms unbonded under 5TER, because 5TER removes them from
the template.

That is the same wall as above, met from the other side. 5TER can bond the
strand but not keep its phosphate; 5PHO can keep the phosphate but not end the
chain. So 1EFS cannot be given a CHARMM36 reference in this file as it stands
-- either its terminal phosphate is removed, or O5T and H5T are added to it
and the chain end stops being expressible.

The RNA control has a 5-prime hydroxyl and does not have this problem, which
is why the reference above is built on it.

C-09  The deck converts from the System; the engine cannot load it yet   PART
------------------------------------------------------------------------
tools/openmm_system_to_mosaics.py reads the built System rather than the XML,
because charmm36_2024.xml declares no impropers -- they are generated when it
loads, so a converter reading the file writes a deck missing a term.

What it reads, on the RNA control:

    bonds                    136        angles               241
    Urey-Bradley              42        harmonic impropers    10
    torsion types            273        1-4 pairs            320
    Lennard-Jones classes     30        types carrying it    127

ACCEPTANCE IS THE ROUND TRIP, NOT THE DECK APPEARING. Every parameter the
System holds is rebuilt from the per-type tables the deck is written from:

    1-4 sigma       4.440892e-16 Angstrom
    1-4 epsilon     5.888381e-16 relative

Two things that check caught, both of which produce a deck that looks fine:

  The Lennard-Jones is indexed by CLASS, not by atom type. There are 30
  classes and 127 types. Taking one representative type per class left 97
  types with no Lennard-Jones at all.

  No 1-4 pair has the same atom type at both ends -- a type is a residue and
  an atom, and an atom is never three bonds from itself -- so recovering the
  per-type 1-4 parameters by seeding on matching type names finds nothing and
  every type silently falls back to its ordinary Lennard-Jones. Classes do
  repeat, and all 30 recover.

THE RTF, AND WHERE THIS STOPS

write_rtf emits the MASS table, one RESI block per residue with the charges
read from the System, the bonds from the topology, and the CHIGROUP/CHIPRIM
natural-move lines carried across from the shipped RTF. Those describe the
molecule rather than the force field, and MOSAICS uses the same atom names for
a nucleic residue whichever deck is loaded, which is C-06.

MOSAICS reads both files. It then stops, and the reason is the structure this
deck was generated from rather than the deck:

    RA 1 was not found in .rtf

The engine resolves RA5 to the internal RA and needs both defined: the shipped
RTF carries RESI RA and RESI RA5 and RESI RA3. The RNA control is a 4-mer in
which every base appears once and two of them are terminal, so it cannot
supply an internal ADE at all.

Closing this needs the deck generated from a structure containing every base
in every position it will be used in, which is a longer strand, not a change
to the converter.

WHAT IS STILL NOT DONE

The rest of the deck. This closes the question of what a converted deck would be checked
against; it does not convert one. The converter still reads the XML, and
charmm36_2024.xml declares no impropers there -- they are generated at load
time -- so a deck for this release has to be read out of the built System
rather than out of the file.


C-06  Nothing has to be renamed                                    NOTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Worth recording because it makes (b) and (c) smaller than they look. MOSAICS
already uses CHARMM atom naming for internal nucleic residues: O1P and O2P,
not OP1/OP2; C5M/H51/H52/H53 for the thymine methyl; and the CHARMM 2-prime
convention where H2' is the hydroxyl hydrogen on O2' and H2'' is the carbon
hydrogen on C2'.

Diffing examples/pure_terminal_controls/dna_acgt.pdb against the CHARMM36
templates with DEOX applied:

    DA5 vs ADE+DEOX   only HO5' against the phosphate CHARMM keeps
    DC  vs CYT+DEOX   no difference
    DG  vs GUA+DEOX   no difference
    DT3 vs THY+DEOX   only HO3'

Every difference is terminal. Internal residues need no renaming at all.


C-07  The deck would be 30 MB                                      NOTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
write_nonbonded emits the full upper triangle over all types. 412 types gives
85,078 lines in each of .onfo and .vdw, 15.8 MB and 15.0 MB. A nucleic-only
deck needs about 39 types, which is 780 pairs each.

Restricting the emitted types is worth doing before any CHARMM36 deck becomes
a committed artefact. It is not done here because there is no deck to commit.

C-10  The deck loads, and the RNA control matches OpenMM             FIXED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-09 stopped because the 4-mer never defined an internal ADE, so the
engine resolved RA5 to RA and died. The deck is now generated from an
8-mer (A5 C G U A C G U3) that carries every base internally as well as
at the ends that the control uses, then checked on the committed 4-mer.

Two further gaps, both in the converter, not in the force field:

  MOSAICS builds a torsion for every bonded 1-4 and dies if the deck has
  no row. CHARMM omits an unparameterised quadruplet (energy 0). The
  converter now writes that 0. 98 rows on the 8-mer. Without them the
  engine stopped on ADE-C4' ADE-C3' ADE-C2' ADE-C1', a sugar ring
  torsion OpenMM never applied.

  MOSAICS identity classifies a 5-prime hydroxyl by HO5', CHARMM by H5T.
  The RTF now spells HO5'/HO3' and keeps the CHARMM type. IMPROPER lines
  are written from the System's harmonic impropers.

C-11  The combined DNA+RNA deck, and the DNA control matches OpenMM     FIXED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-10 closed the RNA 5-OH path on a 6-residue deck that never defined a
DNA residue. The deck is now generated from the 4-mers in
work/charmm36-na/oligos, which carry every base as 5-prime, internal and
3-prime, DNA and RNA:

    DA5 DA DA3  DC5 DC DC3  DG5 DG DG3  DT5 DT DT3
    RA5 RA RA3  RC5 RC RC3  RG5 RG RG3  RU5 RU RU3

24 residues. ADE/CYT/GUA are the same CHARMM template for both sugars;
DEOX is a patch. No O2' means deoxyribose, so those become DA/DC/DG,
not RA/RC/RG. tools/openmm_system_to_mosaics.py names them that way
when it writes the RTF.

Three further gaps, all in the converter / reference tool, not in the
engine:

  A 3-prime thymine matches two templates in charmm36_2024.xml:
  THY+DEOX+3TER and URA+DEOX+3TER+5-methyl. OpenMM will not choose.
  The reference builder now prefers the template whose name starts
  with the CHARMM residue assigned to that position, so DT3 is THY.

  MOSAICS still builds a torsion for every bonded 1-4 and dies if the
  deck has no row. The converter still writes energy 0 for those
  quadruplets. 197 rows on the 24-residue deck.

  OpenMM's PDB reader already bonds residues in its standard table.
  DC and DG are in that table; RA5 and RU3 are not. Adding CHARMM
  template bonds on top of those doubled every DNA HarmonicBondForce
  term (37.42 kcal/mol against MOSAICS 18.92). The unique topology
  was already the CHARMM 135 bonds; the force was not. The reference
  builder now drops the PDBFile bonds and rebuilds from the templates,
  which is what the RNA path already did because the reader knew none
  of those residue names. RNA is unchanged.
